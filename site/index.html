Silicon cavities for colour centres and fast electrons, modelled with quasinormal modes.

I prepared this study for the evaluation of my application to the PhD scholarship in Silicon Quantum Electrodynamics at the Deep Nanoscale (DTU Electro, MSCA doctoral network QUEST). The project designs silicon cavities with deep-subwavelength confinement, describes them with modal expansions of the electromagnetic field, and connects them to fast electrons in a transmission electron microscope and to embedded colour centres. Each step is built here at small scale and checked against an exact or published result. Every number on this page comes from the code in the linked repository. PDF in the top bar shows the publication figures instead of the interactive plots.

Purpose and scope

For the evaluators. The call asks for the theory, design and modelling of silicon cavities with deep-subwavelength confinement, using numerical simulations and modal expansions; designs with the specifications that fabrication needs, and models to interpret the measurements; the interaction of cavity fields with fast electrons for transmission-electron-microscope experiments; and the quantum optics of such cavities with embedded colour centres. Modules A–D build each step at small scale, in two dimensions where the PhD would work in three, and test it against an exact or published result. The aim is to show each method built, tested and understood: what agrees, how well, and where the model stops.

Built for this study

  • A 2D finite-element Maxwell solver (Nédélec elements of any order, NGSolve [12]) with perfectly matched layers, a shift-and-invert search for the QNMs, and their PML normalisation $\mathbf e^{T}\mathsf M\mathbf e = 1$ [8].
  • QNM expansions of the transmission of a barrier, of the Purcell factor and of the energy loss of a passing electron, each compared with a direct solution.
  • First-order QNM perturbation theory for moving boundaries (hole radii and positions, bowtie bridge), used for fabrication tolerances.
  • A Lindblad master equation for a two-level emitter and one cavity mode, with the coupled-mode frequencies; unit tests run in CI on every push.

How each step would grow in the PhD

  • 3D: the same QNM workflow for the full membrane, with its out-of-plane radiation and a real Q, and mode volumes without the effective-index step.
  • Design: the perturbation sensitivities of module B are the gradients that inverse design needs; topology-optimised cavities as in [4].
  • Electrons: 3D trajectories near and through the membrane, coupling constants for the electron-microscopy experiments of the network, and their statistics.
  • Emitters: phonon sideband and spectral diffusion of the G centre, several QNMs, and the coupled QNM frequencies of [5] computed from the 3D field.
A

Quasinormal modes, checked against exact results

Vacancy task: modal expansions of electromagnetic fields. A quasinormal mode (QNM) solves Maxwell's equations without sources and radiates outwards; its frequency $\tilde\omega = \omega - i\gamma$ is complex and $Q = \omega/2\gamma$. Normalised QNMs expand the response of a resonator [1]. Before using them on a cavity I check each piece against an exact result: the dielectric barrier of [1] in 1D, analytically; a 2D finite-element solver against the exact QNMs of a silicon cylinder; and the Purcell factor from the QNMs against the direct solution with a point source.

The dielectric barrier of [1] ($n = \pi$)

With the three QNMs $n$ = 3, 4, 5 the error of the transmission has its minimum, , at $\omega L/c$ = 4 (one QNM: ); [1] reports about 2% and 7%. With $2N+1$ QNMs the error falls as $(2N+1)^{-2}$ for the expansion of the total field and as $(2N+1)^{-1}$ for the scattered field. A 2D QNM solver with perfectly matched layers

Silicon cylinder ($n$ = 3.48, $R$ = 0.5 µm) in air, in-plane electric field. The exact QNMs are the roots of $J_m'(nkR)/[nJ_m(nkR)] = H_m'(kR)/H_m(kR)$. The finite-element QNMs converge exponentially with the element order, to at order ( unknowns), for modes with $Q$ from 10 to 14 000.

Purcell factor from a QNM pair

A dipole 80 nm inside the rim of the cylinder, near the $m$ = 4 QNM ($Q$ = ). The QNMs of the cylinder come in degenerate pairs, so the single-resonance model uses both. At the peak, the pair reproduces the direct solution to (radial) and (azimuthal); away from it, the azimuthal dipole also couples to modes the pair leaves out.

Method and result

  • 1D: analytic QNMs $\tilde\omega_mL/c = [2\pi m + i\ln((n-1)^2/(n+1)^2)]/2n$, with field $e^{in\tilde k_mx} + (-1)^m e^{-in\tilde k_mx}$ inside and norm $(-1)^m2n^2L$ [1]. Total field: $E \approx \sum_m a_m\tilde f_m/(\omega - \tilde\omega_m)$, with $a_m$ from the incoming field at the two surfaces; scattered field: $b_m = -\tfrac{\omega}{2}\int\tilde f_m\,\Delta\varepsilon\,E_{\rm in}\,dx$.
  • 2D: curl–curl equation in Nédélec elements, a radial PML (absorption α = 2i), shift-and-invert Arnoldi on $(\mathsf A - \sigma\mathsf M)^{-1}\mathsf M$; spurious PML modes are rejected by their weight in the PML.
  • Purcell factor: power of a 4 nm source disc, relative to the same source in homogeneous silicon; QNM model $\mathbf e = \sum_n \mathbf e_n(\mathbf e_n^T\mathbf f)/(\tilde k_n^2 - k^2)$.
B

A silicon cavity for the G centre, with and without a bowtie

Vacancy task: design silicon nanostructures with the specifications fabrication needs, and models to interpret the measurements. The starting point is the nanobeam with which [2] enhanced the emission of a single G centre: 220 nm silicon-on-insulator, holes of radius 115 nm and period 350 nm, a four-hole taper to 85 nm and 250 nm, beam width 520 nm (symmetric here, with six mirror holes on each side). I model the membrane in 2D with the effective index $n_{\rm eff}$ = of its TE0 slab mode, from $n_{\rm Si}$ = at 1278 nm [11], and scale all in-plane dimensions to put the cavity mode on the G-centre line. A silicon bowtie at the centre, left between two air trapezoids that replace the innermost holes, then concentrates the field in a bridge of width $w$, where the emitter sits.

C

Fast electrons passing the cavity

Vacancy task: light–matter interaction between cavity fields and fast electrons, for the design and interpretation of electron-microscope experiments. An electron moving at speed $v$ on a straight line exchanges energy with a mode through the Fourier component of the field at $q = \omega/v$ along its path [9]. I take an aloof trajectory in the plane of the membrane, parallel to the beam at a distance $b$ from its edge; in 2D the electron is a line charge. Two quantities: the coupling integral $C(v) = \int\tilde f_x(x, y_e)\,e^{-i\omega x/v}dx$ of the normalised QNM, which sets the electron–photon coupling in photon-induced near-field electron microscopy [10], and the energy-loss spectrum, computed directly (scattered-field FEM driven by the field of the moving charge) and from the single QNM.

Coupling versus electron energy

The coupling peaks at keV (nanobeam) and keV (bowtie), for all three distances, and falls as $b$ grows. Above the peak the bowtie keeps much more coupling: at 300 keV, of its maximum against for the nanobeam. $C(v)$ is the spatial spectrum of the field at $q = \omega/v$, and the field concentrated at the bowtie has a broader spectrum.

Phase matching

The spatial spectrum of $\tilde f_x$ along the trajectory peaks at $q$ = π/a (nanobeam), next to the band edge of the mirror. Phase matching $v = \omega a/\pi$ gives keV, close to the computed optimum.

Energy loss near the resonance

At the energy of best coupling and $b$ = 50 nm. At the peak the single QNM agrees with the direct solution to (nanobeam) and (bowtie). The direct spectrum is positive at every frequency, as it must be for a passive structure.

Method and result

  • Electron field: in vacuum, $\phi \propto e^{iqx-\kappa|y-y_e|}$ with $\kappa = q/\gamma$; it drives the scattered field through $k^2(\varepsilon-1)\mathbf E_0$ inside the silicon.
  • Loss: $\Gamma(\omega) \propto -{\rm Re}\int dx\,e^{-iqx}E^{\rm ind}_x(x, y_e)$ along the trajectory inside the computational domain.
  • Limits: a line charge, not a point electron; an aloof path in the plane, not through or across the membrane.
D

A G centre in the cavity

Vacancy task: quantum optics of the cavities with embedded colour centres. The G centre in silicon emits at 1278 nm, with a lifetime of 5.97 ns in the nanobeam outside the cavity, a Debye–Waller factor of 15% and a quantum efficiency ε of 1–10% [2]. The zero-phonon rate $\Gamma_{\rm ZPL} = {\rm DW}\,\varepsilon\,\Gamma_0 = n\omega^3\mu^2/(3\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c^3)$ gives the dipole moment, μ = D (ε = 1%) to D (10%), and a mode volume $V$ gives $\hbar g = \mu\sqrt{\hbar\omega/2\varepsilon_0 n^2 V}$. The emitter and the mode then follow the Lindblad master equation of the Jaynes–Cummings model, with cavity loss κ = ω/Q, emitter decay γ = 1/τ0 and pure dephasing γ*.

Reading the measurement of [2]

  • The Purcell factor of [2], $F_P = \tau_0(1/\tau_{\rm on} - 1/\tau_{\rm off})/({\rm DW}\,\varepsilon)$, with its lifetimes τ0 = 5.97, τon = 0.97 and τoff = 4.27 ns and DW = 15%, gives FP = /ε: their lower bound of 31 for ε = 1.
  • FP cannot exceed (3/4π²)Q/V = for their cavity, so the measurement implies ε ≥ : for ε = 1% the cavity would have to enhance the zero-phonon line by more than it can.
  • A check of the code: the coupled-mode eigenvalues equal those of the Liouvillian in the one-excitation coherences (unit test).

Evidence, simplifications and references

Simplifications

  • The cavity is modelled in 2D (top view of the membrane, effective index of the TE0 slab mode, constant silicon index); Q is the in-plane value, and V = A heff is an estimate.
  • The designs are scaled in-plane to put the mode at 1278 nm in this model; the scale factor would differ in 3D.
  • The electron is a line charge on an aloof path in the plane of the membrane.
  • The G centre is a two-level system with published lifetime, Debye–Waller factor and a range of quantum efficiencies; no phonon sideband dynamics, no spectral diffusion.
